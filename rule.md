# Kuhhandel / You’re Bluffing!

Kuhhandel is Rüdiger Koltze’s Ravensburger auction and bluffing card game, also published in English as *You’re Bluffing!*. It is for 3–5 players, ages 10+, and takes about 45 minutes.

## Goal

Collect complete four-card animal sets. At the end, the player with the highest score wins. Money has no value at the end.

## Components

The classic second edition contains:

- 40 animal cards: 10 species, four cards per species.
- 55 money cards: 10 × 0, 20 × 10, 10 × 50, 5 × 100, 5 × 200, and 5 × 500.
- A card tray and one spare card.

The number on an animal card is the value of its complete four-card set:

| Animal | Set value |
| --- | ---: |
| Rooster | 10 |
| Goose | 40 |
| Cat | 90 |
| Dog | 160 |
| Sheep | 250 |
| Goat | 350 |
| Donkey | 500 |
| Pig | 650 |
| Cow | 800 |
| Horse | 1,000 |

## Setup

1. Shuffle the animal cards and place them face down in the tray.
2. Sort the money cards by value. Give each player two 0 cards, four 10 cards, and one 50 card. Keep money hidden.
3. Put the remaining money in the tray. Choose a starting player; play moves clockwise.
4. Keep acquired animal cards face up so everyone can see them.

## A turn

The active player chooses one action:

1. Auction the top animal card.
2. Offer a Kuhhandel to a player who owns at least one animal of the same species.

Kuhhandel is not possible until a species is split between players. After the action, the next player takes a turn.

## Auction

1. The active player reveals the top animal card and acts as auctioneer. The auctioneer does not bid.
2. Other players bid aloud. Each bid must be higher than the last.
3. If nobody bids, the auctioneer takes the animal for free.
4. Otherwise, the highest bidder wins. Immediately after the final bid, the auctioneer may use the right of first refusal: pay that bid to the highest bidder and take the animal. If the auctioneer declines, the highest bidder pays the auctioneer and takes the animal.

There is no change. Pay with money cards, using a higher total when the exact amount cannot be made. A player who cannot cover a winning bid must show all money; remove that player from the auction and repeat the auction without them. The same rule applies if the auctioneer cannot pay when using first refusal.

If no animal cards remain, players must make any available Kuhhandel before the game can end. A player who owns only complete sets is skipped.

## Kuhhandel

The active player is the challenger. The other player cannot refuse.

1. The challenger places any number of money cards face down. 0 cards may be used to bluff.
2. The other player chooses one option:
   - Accept: take the hidden offer and give the required animal card or cards.
   - Counteroffer: place a hidden bid. Both players reveal their bids at the same time.
3. The higher bid wins the opponent’s required animal card or cards. In the second edition, the players exchange their revealed bids.
4. If the bids tie, repeat the hidden bids in the same order. If the second bids also tie, the challenger takes the animal for free.

0 cards used in a revealed bid are returned at once.

The trade covers one card if either player owns only one card of that species. If both players own a pair, the trade covers both cards. A player with all four cards of a species cannot be challenged for it.

## Donkey bonus

When a donkey is revealed, pause the auction and give every player extra money before continuing:

- First donkey: 50
- Second donkey: 100
- Third donkey: 200
- Fourth donkey: 500

The donkey is then auctioned normally.

## End and scoring

The game ends when all animal cards have been auctioned and no Kuhhandel remains.

For each completed species, use its set value. Add those values, then multiply the sum by the number of completed species.

Example: pigs (650), dogs (160), and roosters (10) give `(650 + 160 + 10) × 3 = 2,460` points.

## First-edition differences

Some older copies contain 25 × 10 cards, 5 × 50 cards, and a blue dove card worth 500 instead of the second edition’s 20 × 10 cards, 10 × 50 cards, and spare card.

For that edition:

- Give each player two 0 cards and five 10 cards at setup.
- In a Kuhhandel, the winner’s bid goes to the loser; the loser takes their own bid back. The bids are not exchanged.
- The first player to complete all four roosters and geese, cats and dogs, or sheep and goats takes the dove card.
- The normal first-edition score is the sum of completed set values plus the dove bonus. The multiplier above is an optional professional variant that does not use the dove.
